S1 cross-model check on the development split (dev-400)

Why this exists (decision 0034): a single model scoring 8.7% could mean the task is hard or
it could mean our model is weak. A second model from an unrelated family, given the same
evidence and the same output format, separates the two. The check is not looking for a
better model -- it is looking for evidence that the bar is the task.

WHAT THE COMPARISON SAYS

  Luna (openai/gpt-5.6-luna)      top-1  8.7%   top-3 22.9%   abstain 22.9%   0/401 failed
  Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite           top-1 12.5%   top-3 22.9%   abstain  1.5%   0/401 failed

  Top-3 is identical to the decimal. Two model families, trained separately, reach the same
  place on the same evidence. That is the result the check was run for: 8.7% is the task
  being hard, not Luna being weak.

  The top-1 gap is temperament, not judgement. Gemini abstains on 1.5% of cases where Luna
  abstains on 22.9%: it guesses where Luna declines. Restricted to the cases each model
  chose to answer, they are 11.3% (Luna) and 12.7% (Gemini) -- the same within noise.

  Cost is not comparable between the two rows. Gemini ran on the standard (immediate) price
  because its batch submission sat unstarted for eleven hours; Luna ran on the batch price.
  Per case: Luna $0.0011, Gemini $0.0024, roughly the 2x endpoint difference.

TWO MODELS THAT COULD NOT RUN, recorded because the check's value depends on them

  google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite  refused every request: "Requests ending with a model turn
    are not supported". Our stage 2 sends the stage-1 reply back as an assistant turn and
    asks for a refinement, so the request ends on a model turn. Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite
    accepts the same shape; this is a version cut, not a family one.

  z-ai/glm-5.3-flash  could not answer inside our 2,000-token output cap. 376 of 401
    stage-1 replies were unusable: 287 finished at exactly the cap with EMPTY content,
    89 truncated mid-answer, 25 clean. It deliberates internally and spends the whole
    output budget before emitting anything. "flash" in a model name does not mean
    non-reasoning. Not re-run at a raised cap: a model given a different token budget is
    not a like-for-like comparison, and the point of the check is the bar, not the model.

  The general lesson (0031 point 4): our output cap excludes far more of the market than
  the word "reasoning" suggests, and neither the model's name nor its price predicts it.
  Any future model comparison must probe finish_reason on a REPRESENTATIVE sample before
  committing spend -- our smoke test took the FIRST 10 cases of a sorted file, which are
  short enough to pass, and so reported 10/10 while the true failure rate was 94%.
